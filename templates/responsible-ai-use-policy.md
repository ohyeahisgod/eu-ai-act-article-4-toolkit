## 1. Purpose

This policy sets the minimum rules for selecting, configuring and using AI systems at `[organisation]`. Its purpose is to support safe and accountable use while keeping decisions, learning measures and evidence connected to the systems in use.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, temporary workers, contractors and service providers who select, build, configure, operate, review or govern AI systems on behalf of `[organisation]`.

It covers approved AI systems recorded in `[inventory location]`. Personal experiments using organisational information or conducted for organisational work are also in scope.

## 3. Ownership

- The accountable executive approves risk appetite and resources.
- The AI governance owner maintains the inventory, policy, review calendar and evidence trail.
- System owners document intended purpose, users, data, limitations, affected people, oversight and changes.
- Users follow approved-use instructions and report unexpected behaviour.
- Human reviewers retain authority and time to challenge, override or stop AI-supported outcomes.

## 4. Approved systems and purposes

Only systems and purposes listed in `[approved-system register]` may be used for organisational work. A new system or material new purpose requires review before use.

Minimum approval record:

- provider and organisational role;
- intended purpose and actual users;
- data categories and affected people;
- known limitations and risk status;
- accountable owner and human oversight;
- required learning measures; and
- next review date and change triggers.

## 5. Information handling

Users must not enter personal, confidential, client, security-sensitive or legally privileged information unless the system, environment and purpose have been explicitly approved for that information.

Users must follow `[data classification policy]`, apply data minimisation and use only authorised accounts and integrations. Suspected disclosure or unauthorised retention must be reported through `[incident channel]` immediately.

## 6. Output verification and human review

AI output is not automatically correct or authoritative. Users must verify material facts, calculations, sources and instructions before relying on or distributing output.

For decisions affecting people, `[organisation]` will define who performs human review, what information they receive, when they must override or escalate, and how the decision is recorded. Reviewers must not merely confirm an AI result without meaningful assessment.

## 7. Customer and public communication

Teams must follow applicable transparency, disclosure, accessibility and contractual requirements. AI-generated or manipulated content must be labelled where required by law or `[organisation]` policy.

Customer-facing output must have a named escalation path. Complaints or evidence of harm must be recorded and routed to `[owner]`.

## 8. Prohibited internal uses

Without documented approval, users must not:

- use unapproved AI systems for organisational work;
- submit restricted information to public or consumer AI services;
- allow AI output to make a consequential decision without required human review;
- impersonate a person or conceal material AI use where disclosure is required;
- bypass security, procurement, privacy or records-management controls; or
- continue using a system after a stop-use instruction or material incident.

This internal list is not a legal classification of prohibited AI practices under the EU AI Act.

## 9. AI literacy measures

The governance owner will maintain a role-based matrix that considers organisational role, system context and risk, people's existing knowledge and experience, and affected people.

Evidence may include dated guidance, scenario exercises, assessments, acknowledgements, observed practice, material versions and review records. A certificate alone is not treated as proof that a measure fit the context.

## 10. Incidents and escalation

Report unexpected behaviour, material errors, bias, unsafe recommendations, data exposure, security issues, misuse or complaints through `[incident channel]`.

The system owner may pause use while `[incident owner]` determines containment, notification, remediation, evidence preservation and whether learning measures or controls must change.

## 11. Monitoring and review

Review this policy and connected controls on schedule and after:

- a new AI system or material purpose change;
- significant provider or model changes;
- an incident, complaint or monitoring concern;
- changes to affected people, data or oversight;
- updated law, official guidance or contractual duties; or
- evidence that users do not understand or follow the controls.

## 12. Evidence retained

`[organisation]` retains the current inventory, role mapping, policy versions, learning content, completion or acknowledgement records, assessment results where appropriate, governance decisions, incidents and review history according to `[retention schedule]`.
