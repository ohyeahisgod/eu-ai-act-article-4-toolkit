# A 30-day Article 4 operationalisation guide

This guide organises work; it is not a legal deadline, compliance certification or substitute for qualified advice. Adjust the sequence to the organisation's size, sector, systems and risk.

## Days 1–5: establish scope and inventory

1. Name an accountable executive and working owner.
2. Record the provider/deployer question for every AI system.
3. List intended purpose, actual use, users, data and affected people.
4. Record human oversight, known limitations and current risk status.
5. Escalate uncertain territorial, role and risk-classification questions.

**Exit evidence:** an owned inventory with no unnamed systems and a dated list of questions requiring qualified review.

## Days 6–10: map people to real exposure

1. Identify who builds, configures, operates and reviews each system.
2. Include contractors and service providers acting on the organisation's behalf.
3. Separate leaders, governance owners, technical builders, operators, human reviewers and customer-facing teams.
4. Record existing knowledge, experience, education and relevant training.
5. Identify people who can affect or are affected by consequential outcomes.

**Exit evidence:** every in-scope system has an owner, users, reviewers and an escalation path.

## Days 11–15: design context-specific measures

1. Establish a plain-language AI foundation where needed.
2. Add system purpose, limitations, correct use and failure modes.
3. Add information handling, verification and incident escalation.
4. Give builders lifecycle, evaluation, monitoring and change-control depth.
5. Give human reviewers practical oversight, override and decision-record exercises.
6. Confirm additional requirements for known or potentially high-risk systems with qualified owners.

**Exit evidence:** a matrix explains why each audience receives its selected depth and topics.

## Days 16–20: publish rules and deliver measures

1. Approve a responsible-use policy tied to the system inventory.
2. Deliver guidance, workshops, scenarios or assessments appropriate to each audience.
3. Provide system-specific job aids at the point of use.
4. Ensure reviewers can practise challenge, override and escalation.
5. Make incident and question channels easy to find.

**Exit evidence:** current content versions, delivery dates, owners and results are recorded.

## Days 21–25: connect the evidence

For a sample person, confirm a reviewer can follow:

`person → role → AI system → risk/context → learning measure → result → policy version → next review`

Record gaps as owned actions rather than silently filling fields with assumptions.

**Exit evidence:** the sample trail can be followed without interviewing the programme owner.

## Days 26–30: test and schedule maintenance

1. Ask one operator to explain permitted use, verification and escalation.
2. Ask one reviewer to demonstrate meaningful oversight on a realistic scenario.
3. Ask one system owner to show the current inventory and change history.
4. Record scheduled reviews and event-based triggers.
5. Present unresolved material risks and resource needs to accountable leadership.

**Exit evidence:** failed tests create dated actions with owners and due dates; successful tests retain proportionate evidence.

## Ongoing review triggers

- new system or use case;
- material provider, model or configuration change;
- incident, complaint or monitoring concern;
- change in data, affected people or human oversight;
- user evidence showing a knowledge or practice gap;
- new law, official guidance or contractual obligation.
